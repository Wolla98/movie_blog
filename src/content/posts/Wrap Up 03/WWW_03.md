# Wrap Up

Well, it's not so weekly at this point. But hey, I've watched some movies, and I've got some thoughts about them. As always, a **Weak** means that the score might change in the future, but a **Strong** means I'm fairly certain about that score right now.

## Stand and Deliver (1998)

I remember watching this movie in my own AP Calculus class back in high school. I guess that's a pretty common experience because a lot of the reviews on letterboxd mention that as well. Unlike those reviews, I actually really like math. I also had a very cool calculus class and a great teacher. Watching this again definitely brought back some memories for me. 

The pacing of the movie felt a little jerk-y. At the start, things are very slow and gradual as the characters warm up to each other. Each scene is a sort of "time skip" so the characters become more familiar with each other in a natural way. But, as the movie goes on, the time between each scene stretches longer to the point where whole months go by. I think a more even pacing would better sell the relationship between the teacher and the students. 

Some plot points felt contrived to pull at your heartstrings, but most scenes are okay. Not a serious or emotional watch, but good comfy viewing.

**Weak 2.5 / 5**

## The Incredibles (2004)

After watching the Fantastic Four: First Steps, I went to rewatch this movie since people always said how heavily it drew from the comics. I have to say, this moving is fantastic. The family element is much stronger since the characters are an actual family this time. It also leads to some interesting dynamics as the adults and kids deal with different kinds of problems. It also helps that despite being a movie targeted towards families, it never talks down to the audience like they are dumb. It tackles the serious problems and understands that children are smart enough to pick up on deeper themes.

Honestly, I've always believed that the best medium for superheroes is animation. Despite the somewhat dated visuals, the animation style is so expressive and alive that it still looks amazing to this day. And the score is absolutely amazing. Michael Giacchino burned down the studio with how fire the soundtrack is. I still consider this one of Pixar's greatest works to this day

**Strong 4.5 / 5**

## Demon Slayer: Kimetsu no Yaiba Infinity Castle (2025)

I used to be fairly into the anime sphere when I was younger. Because of that, I actually read the Demon Slayer manga all the way through when it was coming out, so I wasn't going into this movie completely blind. I do remember thinking this was the most interesting arc in the manga, though. I feel like this series has the same core appeal as My Hero Academia. It boils the genre all the way down into half emotion, half hype moments. That's good if you're brain is turned off or the movie is on a second monitor, but not so good if you're sitting in a theater and focused on it.

The movie was like 70% flashbacks, which was funny at first but got progressively less funny as the movie went on. Other than the one main flashback character, there were too many hype moments to fit any meaningful character interactions. The result is the movie feeling like when I make my action figures fight each other. Yeah, I have a "personality" attached to each of them, but it doesn't really go deeper than surface level. I would not watch this movie if you're looking for even a decent character drama.

That being said, the animation in this movie is phenomonal. I feel like the deeper I go into my own animation skills, the more incredible these animated movies become to me. The setting of this movie consists of a bunch of complex, repeating interiors that would be terrible to animate frame-by-frame in 2D. So, they did some shockingly good compositing of 2D main characters on mostly 3D backgrounds. It's not so good that you can't tell what is 3D and what is 2D, but there are definitely moments of heavy 3D usage that look as good as the 2D stuff. I've been chasing that look for a long time now, so it brings a tear to my eyes to know that it's finally being done (and not looking terrible). And as always, the 2D art is stylistic, fluid, and never ceases to amaze me.

**Strong 2.5 / 5**

## Incantation (2022)

Meh horror movie. I didn't really feel creeped out, unnerved, scared, grossed out, etc. There's a bit of drama around an awful parent almost obsessively needing to control a child, which I felt was actually very interesting. But the movie seems more interested in paranormal scares.

**Weak 2 / 5**

## Willy's Wonderland (2021)

Sometimes I watch a movie and I enjoy it so much that I feel ashamed to be a critic. Critically, this movie is really bad. It's a low budget "horror" movie where everyone's dialogue is stupid and the monsters are stupid. But, putting on my movie enjoyer hat, this movie is so stupid in a good way. The movie knows that it is a low budget Nicholas Cage movie, so it does what I think is the best thing a movie like this can do. It tries something new, and strives to not be boring. 

Nicholas Cage doesn't say a single word in this movie. It's so stupid, but I love it so much. I can tell that he's enjoying acting as this stoic weirdo through body language alone. There's like a dozen robots, but Nicholas Cage just curbstomps all of them one-by-one as the movie progresses, it's honestly amazing. The other characters, who are well aware of the murder robots, are even horrified that Nicholas Cage is tearing them apart like the Doomguy.

Character backstories are told in like two flashbacks, so you can tell that the movie isn't that interested in that stuff. It's just the mute Nicholas Cage variety hour and I'm here for it. There were also some interesting shots / editing that I enjoyed. I did not enjoy the weird bright lens flare on a lot of the scenes, but you take the good with the bad. The other characters that aren't Nick Cage don't matter to me, and I don't care. This movie is awesome, see it with your friends with a bucket of popcorn and have a laugh. Just don't expect anything scary or emotional.

**Strong 3.5 / 5**
