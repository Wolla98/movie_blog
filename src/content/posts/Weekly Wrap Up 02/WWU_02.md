# Weekly Wrap Up 2

Already one post and the "Weekly" part hasn't been living up to the name. Hey, cut me some slack, I watched a TV show this time around, which is like multiple movies if you think about it.


Did you guys see the IMAX AI film festival thing that got announced recently? I never really understood the whole appeal with AI generated stuff. I have a bit of experience with animation (don't look for it) but I feel like I'm always honing my skills to get closer to the vision I see in my mind. AI stuff always seems like throwing stuff at a dartboard and hoping it generates something close enough to your vision. I might end up watching the films to give them an honest review, though. If I had to wager, I'd say that the films will probably be wholly lacking in interesting camera movement and a sense of "cohesion". It's probably going to be a collection of disparate shots, all technically fine on their own but together don't form any coherent visual story. But hey, maybe it'll surprise me.


## Spider Man: No Way Home (2021)


This movie is definitely made for people who grew up watching spider man and love the character in all his forms. I was not one of those kids. I ended up watching the Raimi spidermen pretty late into high school and I don't think I ever finished the "Amazing" movies. That being said, I am a big fan of the movies I have seen and an even larger fan of the bad spider man movies (shout out Venom).


Despite me not being a superfan, I still loved this film from the bottom of my nostalgia-filled heart. The characters bounce off awkwardly in a way that would be offputting in other movies but somehow works in this one. Andrew Garfield and Tobey Macguire are really good as the spider men. Alfred Molina and Willem Dafoe kill their roles are returning villains. Tom Holland gets to do more dramatic scenes than the first two films and he really kills it. It's a pretty fun time from start to finish.


However, since this was a rewatch, more problems started to pop up that the hype of the first watch obscured. The other villains range from just alright to forgettable. The pacing really starts off slow which is not great. However, the worst part was watching the movie in my house, alone. I try to watch movies as close to the movie experience as possible: giant popcorn bucket and on a large screen. However, there were moments in the film that are so painfully drawn out because the directors expected a laugh or crowd cheering. Especially when the older spider men appear on the screen, it's eerily quiet and long when there's no one cheering or crying in the audience.


So, even though I wouldn't say it's a super good movie, I did like it because I'm a spider man fan. It's a movie that only sets out to farm hype moments and nostalgia, and by god did it do it.


**Strong 3.5 / 5**


## Loki (2021)


Out of all the MCU TV shows, I remember liking Loki the most. So, saddened by the recent showings, I went back to see what I liked about it. I'm glad to say that it did not disappoint. The two seasons had very different tones so I'll go through them separately.


Season 1 was much more grounded in its story. It featured more characters talking about what they want and a lot less of the timey wimey stuff that will come in Season 2. The great thing is that I love when characters just sit around and talk. Loki, despite being a fan favorite, has had shockingly little time to actually be a good character on screen, so I loved having him talk and grow in the first season into a bit of a lovable guy. Owen Wilson as Mobius is absolutely amazing, and it's nice to see a character that can go toe-to-toe with Loki in banter. Sylvie is a nice foil to Loki and is a neat way to show a character learning to love themself. All the side characters were pretty fun too.


One thing that stood out about Season 1 is that the soundtrack is actually really good. The combination of the theremin and orchestra with 80s sci-fi sounds is perfect for this show. The music at the hurricane apocalypse and Old Loki's scene are particularly good. The coloring of the show is also very nice. The TVA has a lot of bright oranges and muted grays / browns that really sells the 80s futurist aesthetic. It also highlights when the characters are not in the TVA. The hurricane apocalypse is very gray / industrial like a Walmart. Lamentis is a very nice shade of purple with neon lights that I have a soft spot for. It's just a good looking season. The only gripe I have is that sometimes the characters can feel "caged in", like they're standing on a greenscreen stage or The Volume and can't really move or interact with much or it'll mess up the shot.


And of course, He Who Remains is a frightening monster. I always love the smug, all-knowing characters, but there's something so great about one who's so eager to die. His one-episode appearance made me very hopeful for his future outings.


Season 2 is much more high-concept than the first. It really leans hard into the time travel concept, and I think it does it really well. I can see how after the more grounded first season, the more "it just works" magic system of the second season can seem confusing. It works great as a catalyst for the growth of Loki and for interesting plot moments, though. Loki looping in time to try to repair the loom is a favorite episode of mine. And in general, the writing of the season is very tight. There are many things that start as mysteries, but close in on themselves neatly as the show progresses. Loki actually has a ton of dramatic scenes to show how he acts in situations where he's helpless, or smug, or confident, and it really helps to sell the character. The side characters take a hit in writing this season, but it's in service of putting a magnifying glass on Loki's character, so I can accept that.


Compared to Season 1, though, it's apparent that the camerawork is much better. There's a lot more handheld and dolly shots that cut down on the "stiffness" of the shots in Season 1. The camera often follows characters as they walk, zooms in and out for emphasis more often, and sometimes even whippans for comedic effect. The camera definitely sells the more "personal" focus of the season and helps to make this world feel more alive.


I ended up really liking Loki on this rewatch. I can definitely see that it's not a show for everyone, though. It can be a bit slow at times, and I understand character work is not enough for some people. And, if you have a bit of the Cinema Sins plot hole virus, you're definitely going to come away from Season 2 with a bunch of questions. But for me, these were two very good seasons of television.


**Strong 4.5 / 5**


## Kung Fu Hustle (2004)


My first foray into comedy Kung Fu movies. This one was actually really good. The absurdist comedy was very funny to my Gen Z brain. The effects were Spy Kids 3 level bad, but that only adds to the comedy. Some of the action scenes, like the ones with the three masters, are actually really good and showcase some impressive choreography that's not afraid to be a little silly. The fights with The Beast are a little less good because the movie starts to rely on the god awful CG. My favorite fight has to be with the musician assassins though. It's what the *Dr. Strange: Multiverse of Madness* music fight dreams of being.


Despite being translated into english subtitles, I also thought that the movie was pretty funny. My favorite running joke is people dying, and other people telling them to say their last words in Chinese because they can't understand them. I'm a sucker for Rosencrantz and Guildenstern characters and they're pretty good in this one. Some jokes don't age as well, specifically the gay old man one (although that one still works in another joke as a contrast to how cool he is as an actual fighter), but otherwise the comedy still holds up pretty well. And the axe gang is an all-time favorite for a reason. Not only are they cool, but gangsters with a dance routine is very funny. It's even funnier that the guys dance when walking around in the first scene after killing two dudes.


I'm definitely biased because I personally found this movie to be funny, but hey, that's kinda the point of comedy movies. I wouldn't put too much weight into this score as an actual "value" of the movie, just imagine more of a "how much did I enjoy it" score.


**Weak 3.5 / 5**


## Pixels (2015)


It's a pretty generic Adam Sandler movie with standard Happy Madison stuff. The plot is whatever, the characters are whatever, really did not like the annoying side character. It did seem like the people who made the movie had a modicum of respect for games because it had a lot less "games are for losers" than I was expecting. Watch it with your friends and laugh when Paul Blart is on screen.


**Strong 1 / 5**
